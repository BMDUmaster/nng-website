Gemstones · 2 min read

Natural, lab-grown or imitation: what are you choosing?

Three labels that can look similar, but tell different stories about a stone.

Demo editorial • Awaiting Narayani’s review
The short answer

A beautiful stone and a correctly described stone are two different things. Ask about both.

01

Start with the material

Natural describes geological origin. Laboratory-grown gems are made by people and share essentially the chemical composition and properties of their natural counterparts. An imitation resembles another gem but is a different material. These are not interchangeable descriptions.

02

Treatment is a separate question

A natural stone can also be treated. Heat, dyes or other processes may change appearance. Ask what is known about treatment and whether it changes how the piece should be cared for. A label that says natural does not answer every question.

03

Ask before you compare prices

Colour alone cannot settle identity or origin. Compare pieces only after their descriptions are clear.

  • What material am I being offered?
  • Is it natural, laboratory-grown or an imitation?
  • What treatments are disclosed?
  • Is independent identification available for this exact piece?
04

Keep meaning and identification separate

You may value a stone as jewellery, a keepsake or part of a spiritual practice. That meaning does not identify the material. Give yourself room to appreciate the piece while asking practical questions about it.
