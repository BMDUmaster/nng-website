01What are the main systems of numerology, and how do they differ?

Eight are described above: Chaldean, Pythagorean, Vedic or Indian, Tamil or Dravidian, Western modern, Lo Shu, money numerology and house-number numerology. They differ in what they count, names or dates or both, in the letter table, in the planet given to 4 and 7, in whether master numbers are kept, and in which name is used. The table above sets these out.

02What is Chaldean numerology?

The Chaldean table shown here assigns letters values from 1 to 8. The Pythagorean table cycles through 1 to 9. That difference can give a name different totals. Ask which table is being used rather than treating one answer as an error.

03What is Pythagorean numerology?

A method that gives the alphabet the values 1 to 9 in order, so A is 1, B is 2 and I and R are 9. Despite the name, no source ties it to Pythagoras. Many modern pages use it for the birth name, and Western books build their longer lists of numbers on it. It gives different values from the Chaldean table for sixteen of the 26 letters.

04Is Chaldean or Pythagorean numerology more accurate?

No test comparing them was found, so neither can be called more accurate. Readers choose between them by habit, training and the kind of question: many Indian pages use Chaldean values for names, and many Western books use Pythagorean ones. What you can check is whether a reader has applied the chosen table correctly, and whether they say which one it was.

05What is Vedic, or Indian, numerology?

On these pages, Indian numerology refers to birth-day and full-date calculations linked with grahas. The label does not establish Vedic authorship or Narayani's preferred method.
