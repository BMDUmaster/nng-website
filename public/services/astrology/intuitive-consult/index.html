Attention, impressions and the question

An account can contain several layers: what happened, what you thought it meant, what you wanted and what you fear. Keeping those layers separate lets you see which part is a fact and which is an interpretation.

An impression should be specific enough to question. You can ask what supports it, what would count against it and whether another explanation fits the same details. This is a reflection prompt, not Narayani's assessment protocol.

Nothing here claims access to another person's private thoughts. A conversation can explore your experience without deciding what someone else intends.

From a detail to a better question

Attention turns into questions. These six rows show the habit with examples.

A detail you noticeThe question it leads to
The same argument turns up in three different settingsIs the argument about the place, or about something under it?
One person is described with the same word every timeWhat would you call it if you could not use that word?
You answer quickly about the other person and slowly about yourselfWhat would you want if it were only up to you?
A date or a stage keeps coming up: the engagement, the move, the family dinnerWhat changed at that point, and who else was affected?
You say “I should” where you might say “I want”Whose voice is that, yours or someone else’s?
You have already decided, and are asking for permissionWhat would you do if nobody had to agree?

Each question is a way of helping you hear something you have already said. None is meant to catch you out. “I don’t know” is always an acceptable answer, and so is “that doesn’t fit”. A wrong impression, said aloud and corrected, is often as useful as a right one, because it shows you what the situation is not.

When you are still putting the question into words

These are examples of questions that may begin as an unsettled feeling. They do not establish a separate appointment category.

You have no birth details
Many people do not know their time of birth, or have no record of it, and some are asking about a person whose details they cannot get. Nothing here depends on a chart, so the consultation can begin from the question alone.
A relationship dilemma
Whether to stay, whether to speak, whether to say yes, how to answer a family that is pulling two ways. The question is usually less about what will happen than about what you want and what you can live with.
A feeling that something is off
A change you cannot name: a partner who seems different, a conversation that no longer feels right, a decision that looked fine and now sits badly. She helps you put words to it first, then asks what it might be pointing at.
A decision the chart does not settle
A chart can describe a period. It cannot tell you whether to take a particular job in a particular city, or to marry a particular person. When the chart has said what it can, the remaining question is yours, and the conversation takes over.

01Does this page offer a psychic reading?

No psychic method is offered here. An impression is not proof of another person's thoughts or future choices.

02Is a separate intuitive consultation available?

This page does not confirm that appointment category. Ask the team about the available guidance options.

03Can it tell me whether someone will return?

No result is promised. You can reflect on what you know, what you hope and which choices are yours.

04Do I need birth details?

Ask what preparation your chosen consultation needs before sending personal details.

05How can I assess an impression?

Compare it with facts you can check. Ask what would count against it and whether another explanation fits. A statement that feels personal is not automatically evidence.

06Can I ask about a proposal or family relationship?

Describe your question briefly. The marriage and matching pages explain those traditional topics, while your own experience and choices remain central.

07What if I have no clear question yet?

Write down what happened and what you wanted to be different. The enquiry guide helps you prepare a first message without offering a discovery session.
